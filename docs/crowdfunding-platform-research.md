# Crowdfunding software landscape for Slander

**Research date:** September 25, 2026. **Market:** primarily U.S. nonprofit campaigns. Prices below are public list prices in USD where available; processing charges, subscriptions, foreign exchange charges, and donor tips are separate unless stated. Sales quotes and contract terms may differ. This note is product research, not legal or tax advice.

## Decision in one sentence

For the Slander concept in [`PRD.md`](PRD.md), use a **hybrid**: buy donation checkout and donor recordkeeping, then build only the distinctive creator workflow around it. Pure buying cannot implement the documented ad and compensation rules; fully custom donation infrastructure is unjustified before proof of demand and an integration failure. No researched fundraising product documents all of Slander's combined requirements: open creative submission, approval and Instagram publication, exact single-ad donation attribution, donation-based voting, rankings, and weekly creator compensation. Standard peer-to-peer fundraising is a nearby feature, not evidence that these rules are supported. This conclusion is an inference from the linked feature and API documentation, not a vendor guarantee.

**Operating assumption:** Slander curates several nonprofits and approves each nonprofit before a campaign; nonprofits do not self-register. Curation narrows onboarding volume and may improve quality control, but Slander still presents and facilitates donations for multiple charitable organizations. Whether that creates a fundraising-platform registration/reporting obligation depends on the precise payment and solicitation flow and each state in which Slander operates; California has rules specifically addressing charitable fundraising platforms. [California AG platform guidance](https://www.oag.ca.gov/charities/pl)

## What the platform does not solve

1. **Attribution must be a ledger rule, not a marketing tag.** Givebutter exports UTM fields; Fundraise Up allows custom URL/JavaScript fields; GoFundMe Pro tracks source codes with cookie precedence. These are usable *inputs*, but Slander must decide how to handle direct visits, shared links, multiple ad touches, edited donation metadata, refunds, recurring gifts, and fraud. The vendor documentation supports data capture, not Slander's promised “exactly one creative ad” adjudication. [Givebutter export](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/2219206-how-to-export-download-transaction-details), [Fundraise Up custom fields](https://fundraiseup.com/docs/custom-fields-overview/), [GoFundMe Pro source codes](https://prosupport.gofundme.com/hc/en-us/articles/37288766514843-Source-codes)
2. **Creator payouts are a separate system.** These products document transfers to the nonprofit, not a documented turnkey rule to pay an arbitrary creator 10% of donations each week. A custom compensation ledger, creator identity and tax onboarding, payout approval, reversals, and reconciliation remain. Payment processor fees also remain if Slander builds its own UI over a processor. [Givebutter payout methods](https://givebutter.com/pricing), [Fundraise Up donation and refund accounting](https://fundraiseup.com/docs/donations/), [Stripe Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/connect/pricing), [Stripe separate charges and transfers](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers)
3. **Vendor-held checkout does not transfer fundraising compliance to the vendor.** Donation claims, the named charitable beneficiary, what reaches the charity, receipts, creator compensation disclosures, and state fundraising-platform obligations require specific review for this model. The FTC calls for clear disclosures by online giving portals; California's AG has specific fundraising-platform rules. [FTC giving portals](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/online-charitable-giving-portals), [California AG](https://www.oag.ca.gov/charities/pl)
4. **“0% platform fee” can mean donor tips and/or donor-covered processor fees.** It is not a zero-cost payment rail. Givebutter's guarantee with tips on is unusually favorable to the nonprofit but changes the donor checkout; CauseVox's tip model still leaves Stripe/PayPal processing. Compare all-in donor cost and nonprofit net revenue at the same donation mix, not headline platform percentages. [Givebutter pricing](https://givebutter.com/pricing), [CauseVox tip/processing](https://support.causevox.com/en/articles/8706057-0-platform-fee-via-causevox-tip)
5. **The 10% creator payment changes the donor promise.** The FTC says an online giving portal should show who receives the money, what reaches the charity, and when. IRS acknowledgment rules identify the donee and require particular statements for gifts of $250 or more. Whether Slander's proposed split supports the intended donor receipt is a question for tax/fundraising counsel once the legal recipient and payment flow are fixed. A creator who is paid to promote a campaign may also need a clear disclosure of that financial connection. [FTC giving portals](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/online-charitable-giving-portals), [IRS acknowledgments](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-contributions-written-acknowledgments), [FTC influencer guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)
6. **The leaderboard can pay people to optimize the wrong thing.** Under the PRD, views carry 30% of the rank and CTR 40%, while donation count and amount carry 30% combined. More ad spend can inflate raw views, a very narrow audience can inflate CTR, and one donation equals one vote regardless of amount. This is a design inference from the documented weights, not a platform claim. Before automating winner payouts, set equal or accounted-for media budgets, define eligible donors and refund treatment, and test whether the score predicts *incremental net donations*.
7. **A custom payment ledger requires real operations.** Stripe says webhook delivery can be duplicated and arrive out of order; separate charges/transfers put processing fees, refunds, and chargebacks on the platform balance, and a refund does not reverse transfers automatically. An immutable donation-to-ad mapping, idempotent event handling, reconciliation, and a payout reserve are requirements, not polish. [Stripe webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks), [Stripe separate charges and transfers](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers)

## Illustrative economics: one $100 U.S. card donation

Assumptions: no donor covers fees or tips; no taxes, fraud losses, refunds, FX fees, marketing costs, or Slander overhead; the creator's 10% is measured on the original $100 and paid from the funds received by the nonprofit. That last assumption is **unresolved in the PRD** and must be defined with counsel and the beneficiary nonprofit. These are arithmetic illustrations, not vendor quotes or a statement that the fee/payout arrangement is permitted.

| Path | Platform charge | Card processing | Net before creator pay | Net after $10 creator pay |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Givebutter, tips enabled | $0 | $0 to nonprofit under Givebutter Guarantee | $100.00 | $90.00 |
| Givebutter, tips disabled | $3.00 | $3.20 at 2.9% + $0.30 | $93.80 | $83.80 |
| Donorbox Standard, P2P supporter page, with *eligible* Stripe nonprofit rate | $3.95 | $2.50 at 2.2% + $0.30 | $93.55 | $83.55 |
| Custom Slander UI on standard Stripe domestic-card rate | $0 fundraising-platform fee | $3.20 at 2.9% + $0.30 | $96.80 | $86.80 before Connect/payout fees |

Sources: [Givebutter fee schedule and guarantee](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/1512762-givebutter-standard-pricing-explained), [Donorbox P2P and nonprofit processing rates](https://donorbox.org/pricing), [Stripe standard processing](https://stripe.com/pricing), [Stripe Connect fee models](https://stripe.com/connect/pricing). At the **same standard card-processing rate**, custom Stripe checkout saves the 3% Givebutter platform fee when Givebutter tips are disabled: $3 per $100 donation before engineering or payout costs. A nonprofit-approved Stripe rate could change that comparison, depending on the actual merchant account and contract. With Givebutter tips enabled, custom checkout does not win this narrow nonprofit-net comparison. Donor tip experience and conversion could change the result and should be tested on real campaigns.

## Pure buy, hybrid, or fully custom

| Strategy | Call | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pure third-party campaign | **Reject for the stated PRD** | Fastest donation launch, but the researched products do not document Slander's ad approval/publication, compensation-grade single-ad attribution, or weekly creator payout rules. A P2P fundraiser page is not necessarily a creative ad or a vote. |
| Third-party donation rail + Slander application | **Recommended first release** | Slander owns campaign curation, creative submission/review, Instagram workflow, attribution and payout ledger, while a vendor owns checkout, donor receipts/records, transaction processing integration and routine donation operations. Verify exact API behavior and legal role in a live pilot. |
| Full custom fundraising stack atop Stripe or similar processor | **Defer** | Maximizes control but inherits checkout conversion, donor support, recurring gifts, fraud/disputes, reconciliation, receipts, data security, and multi-party payout design. Stripe's standard card charge and possible Connect fees remain. [Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing), [Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/connect/pricing), [separate charges/transfers](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers) |

## Decision matrix for a pilot

Scores are judgments for Slander's *specific* first campaign, not general product ratings. 5 is favorable. “Control” means how much Slander can own the surrounding experience and data integration; it does not imply ownership of processor token vaults or recurring donor payment credentials.

| Path | Launch speed | Donation plumbing | Attribution integration | Brand/control | Cost clarity | Overall pilot fit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Givebutter checkout + custom Slander app | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | **Best first test** |
| Fundraise Up checkout + custom Slander app | 4 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 2 | **Strong technical test; get quote** |
| Donorbox + custom Slander app | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | Fallback / pricing comparison |
| GoFundMe Pro + custom Slander app | 2 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 1 | Enterprise sales evaluation |
| Build donation checkout and platform on Stripe | 1 | 2 | 5 | 5 | 3 | Only after product proof or vendor failure |
| Kickstarter, Indiegogo, investment portal | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 3 | Unsuitable instrument/workflow |

**Pilot acceptance test:** ask Givebutter and Fundraise Up to demonstrate (1) a Slander-controlled, immutable ad ID joined to the vendor's exact donation ID in API/webhook data for a completed gift, (2) a signed success event and a later refund/chargeback correction, (3) export/API reconciliation to the processor payout, (4) the legal recipient of the donation and who issues tax receipts when Slander curates multiple nonprofits, (5) whether the vendor can itself pay creators 10% weekly or Slander must do so separately, (6) complete donor and donation exports and recurring-gift migration if a nonprofit changes vendor, (7) branded embedded checkout including a price quote *without* donor tip prompts, and (8) what, if anything, integrates with Meta/Instagram publishing or ad metrics. Get a clear contract position on Slander's paid creator promotion. If no platform can satisfy the first three, build the attribution and donation front end over a payment processor while still avoiding a custom card vault or homemade payments engine.

**Falsifiable build trigger:** building the whole fundraising stack becomes rational only when a critical, measured product requirement cannot be implemented on any viable platform, or a 2–3 year *net* cost model (including engineers, security, donor support, accounting, platform fees and payment fees) strongly favors building. A percentage fee alone is insufficient evidence: replacing a platform does not remove card-network and processor charges, dispute losses, fraud work, payout operations, or compliance.

**First experiment:** run one curated nonprofit campaign with a small creator cohort using vendor checkout, unique ad identifiers, and human-approved creator settlements. Record gross donations, payment/platform charges, creator pay, paid-media spend, refunds, donor complaints, and staff time. Compare *net incremental funds delivered to the nonprofit* with an ordinary fundraising campaign. If the model fails this test, building a better checkout cannot rescue it. If it succeeds and the donation rail blocks reliable attribution or payouts, that is evidence for building the missing layer.
